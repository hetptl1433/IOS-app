How accurate is each forecast?

To plan tonight's charging you need a guess of each hour's price. This page shows how far off each guess was, over 554 nights. A shorter bar is better.

What "RMS error" means

For every hour, take the miss: the forecast minus the real price. Square each miss, average them, and take the square root. The result is a typical miss in cents per kWh, where big misses count extra. A forecast with a 5¢ RMS error is usually about 5¢ off, and sometimes much more.

Every forecast was scored the same way: made with only the prices known at the time, for each hour up to 7 a.m., and compared with the price ComEd actually billed.

Being more accurate didn't save money

Every AI model beat the day-ahead price on accuracy, yet each one saved a little less when SPARC planned with it. Planning only needs to know which hours are cheapest, and the day-ahead price already ranks the hours well. See the savings.
